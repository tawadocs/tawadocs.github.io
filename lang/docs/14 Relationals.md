## 14.1 Temporal relationals
| Relational | IPA     | Function                      | Gloss |
| ---------- | ------- | ----------------------------- | ----- |
| **mha**    | `m̥͡ma` | *Completed / already*         | PRF   |
| **ha**     | `ha`    | *Incipient / about to happen* | INCP  |
| **be**     | `be`    | *Imminent / soon*             | IMM   |
| **ze**     | `ze`    | *Recurrent / repeated*        | ITER  |
| **wari**   | `waɺi`  | *Frequent / a lot, often*     | FREQ  |
| **he**     | `he`    | *Recent / just occurred*      | JUS   |
| **ho**     | `ho`    | *Delayed / not yet*           | PEND  |
| **źi**    | `ʑi`    | *When / during*               | TEMP  |
* They're free particles and usually go before or after the verb
* You may also use *nihi (X)* where *(X)* is your temporal relational:
	* *sua kamu saún samiǫ nihi wari* -> `1SG-AGT eat.V.PLAT 3SG.F-COM fish-ACC TOP-BACK FREQ`
		* "As for my eating fish with her, (it happens) a lot"
		* ~"I eat fish with her a lot"
* Examples:
	* *A ha kamu* -> `AGT INCP eat.V.PLAT`
		* "(I am) about to eat"
	* *A be kamu* -> `AGT IMM eat.V.PLAT`
		* "(I will) eat soon"
	* *A he kamu* -> `AGT JUS eat.V.PLAT`
		* "(I) just ate"
	* *A ho kamu* -> `AGT PEND eat.V.PLAT`
		* "(I have) not yet eaten"
	* *A mya kamu* -> `AGT PRF eat.V.PLAT`
		* "(I have) already eaten"
	* *A ze kamu* -> `AGT ITER eat.V.PLAT`
		* "(I have) eaten again"
	* *A ho jiteku* -> `AGT PEND pee.V.PLAT`
		* "(I) have not yet peed"
	* *A ze jiteku* -> `AGT ITER pee.V.PLAT`
		* "(I) peed again"
	* *Hona wari jiteku* -> `person-AGT FREQ pee.V.PLAT`
		* "People pee a lot"
	* *Hona wari fofu* -> `person-AGT FREQ speak.V.PLAT`
		* "People often speak"
* Temporal relationals can be used in tandem with aspectual particles
* Examples:
	* *sora′a wari ĉiiro* -> `sky-AGT FREQ water-PRSV`
		* "It still keeps frequently raining"

### 14.1.1 Temporal contextual relational
* This particle is used to introduce temporal context
* It has these forms:
	* *ẑinyi* -> `TEMP-AUG.SUP`
		* "after"
	* *ẑista* -> `TEMP-DIM.SUP`
		* "before"
	* *ieẑi* -> `AUG-TEMP`
		* "circa / near / around"
	* *ienhẑi* -> `DIM-TEMP`
		* "not near / not around"
	* *ólẑi* -> `EXCESS-TEMP`
		* "way later / much later / much after"
	* *ólonhźi* -> `DEFIC-TEMP`
		* "way before / much before"

## 14.2 Dynamic relationals
| Relational | IPA  | Function                                     | Gloss |
| ---------- | ---- | -------------------------------------------- | ----- |
| ao         | `ao` | To possess, exist with, or have              | HAVE  |
| ro         | `ɺo` | To cause, create, or make                    | CAUS  |
| de         | `de` | To receive                                   | REC   |
| fu         | `ɸu` | To make potential real or bring about change | ACT   |
| ea         | `ea` | To move or go                                | MOV   |
* When modifying verbs, their modal form, like *ru* or *eaų* will be used (as in *rų ralanq* ("made to stay awake"))

### 14.2.1 Existential relational
* The existential relational, *ao*, denotes 'existing with,' or in plain English, 'having'
* In constructions like "X has Y," the verbal form *au* is used
	* *sua au ĉi* -> `1SG-AGT HAVE.V.PLAT water`
		* "I have water"
	* *sua au sami* -> `1SG-AGT HAVE.V.PLAT fish`
		* "I have fish"

### 14.2.2 Causation
* Causation is marked by *ro*
* *ro* covers the scope of *make*, *create* and *cause* in English
* Its verbal form is *ru*
* Examples:
	* *sua ru kamu* -> `1SG-AGT CAUS.V.PLAT food`
		* "I make food"
	* *Pól'ikua ruthu kortteisan* -> `paul-PERS-AGT CAUS.V.PLAT-PFV.V.PLAT kill.PROD-3SG.F.POSS`
		* "Paul was the reason she died" / "Paul caused her death"

#### 14.2.2.1 Causal
* Use *ronai* for something in causal terms
* *ronai* means something like "to be (made)"
* Format:
	* `SUBJ-a rųnai X(verb)` -> "Subject is made to do X"
	* `SUBJ-a ronai X(noun)` -> "Subject is made to be X"
	* `SUBJ TOP rųnai X(verb)` -> "As for subject, it is made to do X"
	* `SUBJ TOP ronai X(noun)` -> "As for subject, it is X"
* Examples:
	* *sua rųnai kamu* -> `1SG-AGT CAUS.VLNK-IV eat`
		* "I am made to eat"
	* *sua ronai hakariĉ* -> `1SG-AGT CAUS-IV absurd-N.PLAT`
		* "I am made a fool"

### 14.2.3 Receiving
* *de* is the versatile marker used for giving-receiving relations
* By default, it covers the scope of the English words *get*, *understand*, and *receive*
* Use *deku* or *dekunh* for specifying if the "getting" is metaphorical (e.g., understanding) or literal (e.g., physically receiving)
* Most of the times, *deku* or *dekunh* are not necessary; context is enough
* Examples:
	* *sua de zuǫ* -> `1SG-AGT REC 2SG-ACC`
		* "I get you"
	* *sua dethu mirozun* -> `1SG-AGT REC-PFV.V.PLAT mirror-2SG.POSS`
		* "I got your mirror"
	* *sua dereu nayét sami* -> `1SG-AGT REC-IPFV.V.PLAT 1PL-BEN fish`
		* "I will get fish for us"
* *denai* may be used to mean *give*:
	* *sua denaireu zulla asal* -> `1SG-AGT REC-IV-IPFV.V.PLAT 2SG-DAT house-DEF`
		* "I will give you the house"
* *denh* (`REC-G.INV` but preferred gloss is `GET`) may be used to mean *get*:
	* *sua denhreu nayét asapopokiąl!* -> `1SG-AGT GET-IPFV.V.PLAT 1PL-BEN house-SUPR.big-MOD-DEF`
		* "I will get us the biggest house!"

### 14.2.4 Realisation
* The actualiser, *fu*, is used to denote making change real
* It's like "do" in English
* Examples:
	* *sua fureu irimesun* -> `1SG-AGT ACT-IPFV.V.PLAT work-1SG.POSS`
		* "I will do my work"
	* *sua futhu emonol* -> `1SG-AGT ACT-PFV.V.PLAT picture-DEF`
		* "I finished the picture"
		* "I did the picture"
		* "I worked on the picture"
* Also, you can't use it in constructions like "I finished my food":
	* *sua futhu kamssun* -> `1SG-AGT ACT-PFV.V.PLAT food.1SG.POSS`
		* "I did my food"
		* "I worked on my food"
* In that sentence, *fu* is unable to convey enough information to form a meaningful sentence
* It can also add an 'actually' or 'even' nuance when used like this:
	* *fuppu, zu ni iinq* -> actually, you're bad
	* *zu ni iinq fuhi* -> you're bad, actually
	* *fu irimeru* -> actually (do) work
	* *soa fu shireru* -> even he knows / he does know

### 14.2.5 Movement
* *ea* simply marks movement
* Examples:
	* *sua ea asalla* -> `1SG-AGT MOV house-ALL`
		* "I go to the house"
	* *sua roreome sa′a ea asasunlla* -> `1SG-AGT CAUS-IPFV.REL 3SG.F-AGT MOV house-1SG.POSS-ALL`
		* "I will have her come over to my house"
		* "I will have her at my house"
* For the latter example, this alternative construction is also valid:
	* *sua aureu sa ea asasunlla* -> `1SG-AGT HAVE.V.PLAT-IPFV.V.PLAT 3SG MOV house-1SG.POSS-ALL`
		* "I will have her come to my home"
