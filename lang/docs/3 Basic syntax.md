| Particle | IPA   | Gloss | Note                                         |
| -------- | ----- | ----- | -------------------------------------------- |
| ni       | `ɲi`  | TOP   | New information considered comment           |
| a        | `a`   | AGT   | Marks the subject as a doer                  |
| o        | `o`   | SUFF  | Marks the entity being affected              |
| ǫ        | `õ`  | ACC   | Marks the direct object                      |
| ppu      | `pːu` | INDEP | Turns a bound particle into a free one       |
| hi       | `j̊i` | BACK  | Lets the previous clause act as one unit     |
| nq       | `ɴ`   | INV   | Reverses the semantic content of a word      |
| nh       | `n̼`  | G.INV | Reverses the semantic function of a particle |
| nai      | `nai` | IV    | Reverses valency                             |
| pai      | `pai` | RECP  | Marks reciprocal actions                     |

## 3.2 Accusatives, agents, and sufferers
* Use *ǫ* to mark accusative for words functioning as verbs that have not been marked verbs:
	* *sua kita zuǫ* `1SG-AGT like 2SG-ACC` -> "I like you"
* However, omit *ǫ* if the word is already in its verb form (e.g., *o-words* that accept *(r)u* to turn into an innate verb):
	* *sua kamu sami* `1SG-AGT eat.V.PLAT fish` -> "I eat fish"
		* *kamu* is formed from *kamo* + *-(r)u* -> *kamu*
		* this is only for words that end in *o*; other words, unless they are naturally semantically non-verbs and must be verbalised, never take *-(r)u*
* Use *a* to mark the doer of an action or as an implicit pronoun:
	* *a kamthu hya?* `AGT eat.PFV.V.PLAT Q` -> "Have you eaten?"
	* *sua haterthu* `1SG-AGT run.PFV.V.PLAT` -> "I ran"
* Use *o* to mark an entity being affected by something:
	* *sao korru* `3SG.F-SUFF kill.V.PLAT` -> "She was killed"
* The *a* `AGT` particle only marks the doer; however, it does not necessitate that a verb must follow; instead, any word type can follow, and the sentence is parsed depending on the word type:
	* *sua iitei* `1SG-AGT good-PROD` -> "I am good"

## 3.3 Independentiser and backshift
* *-ppu* and *-hi* are similar but not interchangeable particles
* *-hi* lets a preceding phrase or clause act as one unit, like a variable, and *-ppu* turns a bound morpheme into an unbound one
* Here is how *-hi* would be used:
	* *sua kita zuǫ nihi iią nayét* `1SG-AGT like 2SG-ACC TOP-BACK good-MOD 1PL-BEN`
		* here, the topic particle *ni* takes *-hi* to let the preceding clause *sua kita zuǫ* act as one unit
		* this allows *ni* to topicalise an entire sentence and comment on it with *iią nayét* (good for our benefit)
		* together, it may roughly be translated as: "My liking you is beneficial for us"
* Now, let's take a look at the usage of *-ppu*:
	* *llappu asal no riwakatei* `ALL-INDEP house-DEF GEN think-PROD`
		* "Towards the house of thought"
		* here, *-ppu* lets *-lla* affect the sentence that comes after it
	* *ipappu sua kitarra riburyą* `EPIST-INDEP 1SG-AGT like-NEG book-this.PROX.MOD`
		* "I guess I do not like this book"
		* here, *-ppu* lets the bare evidential *-ipa* act as a standalone particle, indicating uncertainty or belief by default
* Because using *-hi* in such contexts would require the listener to consciously track the sentence beforehand, *-ppu* is used, and vice versa

## 3.4 Reversers or inversers
* There are three inversers:
	* *nq*, *nh*, and *nai*
* *nq* turns a word into its antonym, *nh* turns a grammatical particle into its opposite, and *nai* reverses the valency of a verb
* Examples:
	* *nigonq* `dog` -> *nigo* means 'cat,' but *nigonq* means 'dog'
	* *llanh* `ABL` -> *lla* is the DAT/ALL case, but *llanh* is ABL
	* *korrunai* `die` -> *korru* is a verbal form of *koro* that means 'kill;' *korrunai* means 'die'

## 3.5 Reciprocality
* *pai* is used to denote reciprocals
* It usually attaches to *-o* but in those cases, *-o* is omitted and the form *-ppai* is used, like in *suppai* (*su* + *-o* + *-pai*)
* Here are two examples; in one, it is agentive, and the other sufferer:
	* *suppai kamu* -> `1SG-SUFF.RECP eat.V.PLAT`
		* "I eat myself"
	* *suapai kamu* -> `1SG-AGT-RECP eat.V.PLAT`
		* "I eat by myself"
		* "I eat with myself"
* The latter sentence carries a lonely undertone whereas the former is autocannibalistic
* *pai* is also used in reflexive constructions:
	* *suppai jiteku* -> `1SG-SUFF.RECP pee.V.PLAT`
		* "I pee myself"
	* compare with:
		* *suapai jiteku* -> `1SG-AGT-RECP pee.V.PLAT`
			* "I pee with myself"
			* "I pee by myself"
			* or less commonly "I pee to (e.g., on) myself"

## 3.6 Topic
* *ni* is the quintessential topic particle
* Since Tawa lacks an English-style copular particle, such as *is*, *ni* is extremely important
* Its usage is almost identical to the Japanese **wa** (は, ha)
* Examples:
	* *honmafąl ni iią* -> `person-red.MOD TOP good`
		* "(As for) the red man, (he is) good"
