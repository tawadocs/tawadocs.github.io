## 8.1 Tense
* Tawa has no tense, only aspect

## 8.2 Aspect
### 8.2.1 Particle table
|            | Particle | IPA   | Gloss |
| ---------- | -------- | ----- | ----- |
| **Suffix** | to       | `to`  | PFV   |
| **Suffix** | eru      | `eɺu` | CONT  |
| **Suffix** | iro      | `iɺo` | PRSV  |
| **Suffix** | reo      | `ɺeo` | IPFV  |
* Aspect can be marked on the agent (to denote its state) and on the verb (to denote its time of occurrence)
* Examples:
	* *sua kamuthu* -> `1SG-AGT eat.V.PLAT-PFV.V.PLAT`
		* "I ate"
	* *sua kamueru* -> `1SG-AGT eat-CONT`
		* "I am eating"
	* *sua kamuiro* -> `1SG-AGT eat-PRSV`
		* "I am still eating"
	* *sua kamureo* -> `1SG-AGT eat-IPFV`
		* "I will eat"

## 8.3 Mood
### 8.3.1 Particle table
| Placement                | Gloss     | Particle | IPA    | Function                                                             |
| ------------------------ | --------- | -------- | ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `EPIST-evidence`         | EPIST     | ipa      | `ipa`  | Marks epistemic stance                                               |
| `ke V`                   | IMP       | ke       | `ke`   | Marks an imperative (e.g., *ke kamu!* (eat!)); informal, can be rude |
| `V aiko`                 | IMP       | aiko     | `aiko` | Marks an imperative (e.g., *kamu aiko!* (eat!)); formal              |
| `AGT-atai`               | DES       | atai     | `atai` | Expresses inner want, longing, emotional drive toward an action      |
| `question ya`            | Q         | hyo      | `çʲa`  | Marks a polar interrogative                                          |
| `open-ended question yo` | OPEN.Q    | hya      | `çʲo`  | Marks an open-ended question                                         |
| `OPT-AGT`                | OPT       | are      | `aɺe`  | Marks hope, wish, or regret                                          |
| `statement sai`          | AFFEC.REQ | sai      | `sai`  | Marks a polite request, often affectionate, personal, and intimate   |
| `statement fuka`         | REQ       | fuka     | `ɸuka` | Marks a request (e.g., *hateru fuka* (please run!))                  |
| `statement ana`          | AFFEC     | ana      | `ana`  | Marks warmth, appeal, or connection; affective and personal          |

### 8.3.2 Evidentiality
* *ipa* is a bare evidential
* In its default form *ipappu* it reflects the speaker's uncertainty or belief
* But when a source is added after *ipa*, say, *riwaka* (think), then the epistemic source is more clearly marked
* So, *ipariwaka* would be marking *riwaka* (thinking / thought / mind) as the source, meaning that the information came from your mind or thinking
* Examples:
	* *ipappu sua kamuto* -> `EPIST-INDEP 1SG-AGT eat-PFV`
		* "I guess I ate"
		* "I think I ate"
		* "I believe I ate"
	* *ipaniru zua kamrreu sami* -> `EPIST-see 2SG-AGT eat.IPFV.ACC fish`
		* "It seems that you will eat fish"
		* "It looks like that you will eat fish"
		* "By looking at you and your mannerisms, (I believe) that you'll be eating fish"
	* *sa ni ipariwaka su ni iinqą* -> `3SG.F-AGT TOP EPIST-think 1SG-AGT good-INV-MOD`
		* "She deduced that I was bad"
		* "She rationally came to the conclusion that I was bad"

### 8.3.3 Imperatives
* *ke* and *aiko* are both used for imperatives
* *ke* is harsher and may be ruder
* Examples:
	* *ke kamu* -> `IMP eat`
		* "Eat!"
	* *kamu aiko* -> `eat IMP`
		* "Eat!"

### 8.3.4 Desiderative
* *atai* is a desiderative, but its purpose goes beyond simple desire
* It marks a deep, emotional longing for something specifically
* See the difference between a non-desiderative and a desiderative sentence:
	* *su ni niruatai foŝssun* -> `1SG TOP see-DES friend.1SG.POSS`
		* "I miss my friend"

### 8.3.5 Interrogatives
* *hya* and *hyo* are both used for interrogatives
* *hya* is used for open-ended questions, whereas *hyo* is used for simple polar questions
* But both have collapsed into a general interrogative particle *hya*
* *hyo* may still be used if it is necessary to mark the non-polarity of the question (like asking someone if they're feeling good with a *hyo* in order to show that they need to explain why or why not, though these types of constructions are rare!)
* Examples:
	* *ryo ni fuyu hyo?* -> `this TOP what OPEN.Q`
		* "What is this? (demanding an answer beyond simple negation or affirmation, or picking from a list)"
			* lit. "As for this, (it is) what?"
	* *zua iitei hya?* -> `2SG-AGT good Q`
		* "Are you good? (demanding affirmation or negation)"

### 8.3.6 Optative
* *are* is used for marking optatives
* Examples:
	* *areme sua au asa* -> `OPT-REL 1SG-AGT HAVE.V.PLAT house`
		* "(I) wish that I had a home"
	* *so areme sua kamtthu samil* -> `3SG.M OPT-REL 1SG-AGT eat.PFV.V.PLAT fish-DEF`
		* "He wished that I ate the fish"

### 8.3.7 Requests
* *sai* is used for highly intimate request in an affectionate way
* *fuka* is more detached than *sai*
* It's used for simple, but very polite, requests
* It's used for strangers or when you want to request for something, but not in an intimate or affectionate way
* Examples:
	* *a kamu suyét sai* -> `AGT eat.V.PLAT 1SG-BEN AFFEC.REQ`
		* "(Will you) eat for me, please"
	* *a mha kamu fuka* -> `AGT PRF eat.V.PLAT REQ`
		* "Eat already, please!"

### 8.3.8 Affectionate ending
* *ana* is used to end a statement or anything affectionately
* Examples:
	* *su ni kita zuǫ ana* -> `1SG TOP like 2SG-OBJ AFFEC`
		* "I like you, you know?"
		* "I like you, okay?"
