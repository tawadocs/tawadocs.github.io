## 9.2 Relative clauses
* *me* and *eu* are used for relative clauses
* *eu* is used when describing a stative or equative, but *me* is used when describing an identity
* Example:
	* *honĉalme nirutthu honĉalme samila kamu hon* -> `person-F.PLAT-DEF-REL see.V.PLAT-PFV.V.PLAT person-F.PLAT-DEF-REL fish-DEF-AGT eat.V.PLAT person`
		* "The girl that saw the girl that saw the fish eat a person"
	* *honĉaleu korrumasnigą* -> `person-F.PLAT-REL.COP kill.V.PLAT-AG-cat.MOD`
		* "The girl that is a cat killer"
* *me* can also be used to relativise:
	* *tanílme haterén sallanh euhi sa ni iinqą, iinqą korrumas* -> `reason-DEF-REL run.NMLZ 3SG.F-ABL REL.COP-BACK 3SG.F TOP bad-MOD bad-MOD kill.V.PLAT-AG`
		* ~"The reason we are running away from her is that she is a bad, bad murderer"

## 9.3 Connecting clauses
* Clauses may be connected with *so*, *ato*, *nwa*, or *ę*
* *ę* is a simple additive, much like "and", but if used in the context of a verb, the overt object are not attached to the verb
* *ę* also marks simultaneity, unlike *nwa*
* *nwa* is like an additive, but it also flips polarity
* *ato* is used for linking alternatives, just like *or*
* *mo* is a connective particle; as *monh*, it denotes contrastiveness, but, as *mo*, it can denote either sequentiality (like *then* or *so* in English) or comparativeness (like *than* in English)
* Examples:
	* *sua kikita samiǫ ę tsàma* -> `1SG-AGT love fish-ACC and soup`
		* "I love fish and soup"
	* *sua kikita samiǫ nwa tsàma* -> `1SG-AGT love fish-ACC but soup`
		* "I love fish but not soup"
	* *sua korru sa ato so hya?* -> `1SG-AGT kill.V.PLAT 3SG.F or 3SG.M Q`
		* "Should I kill her or him?"
	* *mo mo, sua kamu samil* -> `then then 1SG-AGT eat.V.PLAT fish-DEF`
		* "But, I'm eating fish"
	* *sua kamu samil, mo zua fofu 'ke kiwinq'* -> `1SG-AGT eat.V.PLAT fish-DEF then 2SG-AGT speak.V.PLAT 'IMP stop'`
		* "I was eating fish until you said: 'stop'"
	* *sua kamu tsàma mo hateronq asazunlla* -> `1SG-AGT eat.V.PLAT soup then walk house-2SG.POSS-ALL`
		* "I will have some soup and then walk to your house"
	* *samil ni iepoki mo samilén* -> `fish-DEF TOP AUG-big than fish-INDEF`
		* "This fish is bigger than the other fishes"
* The semantically inversed form *monh* [mon̼] of *mo* (from *mo* + *-(nh)*) is used like "because" in English
* Examples:
	* *a kitasta atoralós monh iekita eauca imo karabal* -> `AGT like-DIM.SUP apple-INDEF.PL because AUG-like fruit SIM creamfruit-DEF`
		* "I barely like apples because I prefer fruits like the creamfruits (bananas, mangoes, strawberries, and kiwis)"

## 9.4 Similatives
* Use *imo* in the sense of "for example," "such as," or "like"
	* *zua nasų kamu canúnh imo wano* -> `2SG-AGT can.VLNK eat.V.PLAT chain-ABESS SIM time`
		* "You can eat without (a) chain, like time"
		* ~"You can eat freely without any restrictions, like time"
* You can stack grammatical particles on *imo* to yield other particles:
	* *imssta* -> attenuative (`ATT`)
		* *su ni imssta zu* -> `1SG TOP ATT 2SG`
			* "I'm barely like you"
	* *imonh* -> dissimilative (`unlike`)
		* *zua imonh karaba monh a toinqą* -> `2SG-AGT unlike creamfruit because AGT bitter-MOD`
			* "You aren't like creamfruits, because you are (quite) bitter"
	* *im* -> inequative (`INEQ`)
		* *sua im zu* -> `1SG-AGT INEQ 2SG`
			* "I'm absolutely nothing like you"
	* *imnyi* -> equative (`EQ`)
		* *su ni imnyi karabalú - óltoi!* -> `1SG TOP EQ creamfruit-DEF.PL EXCESS-sweet`
			* "I'm a creamfruit - way too sweet!"
* The *imimo* form implies 'just like,' just one notch below equation
	* yes, it can mean equation, but its usually not interpreted that way, as you have *imnyi* for that
