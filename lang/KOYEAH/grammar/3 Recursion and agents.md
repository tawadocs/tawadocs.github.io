## 3.2 Nesting
* Nesting is often just handled by the **X ni Y ga Z** structure:
	* *hateri ngoshi ni kamu mafi atora ga unsunai* :: `fast.ATTR bird TOP eat.V red.ATTR apple NOM live.V-MID`
		* ~"The fast bird that ate the red apple lives"
* Or by the **X a/o Y ga Z** structure, if **X** has to be stated as actor or undergoer:
	* *Sa a aishi foshōesun ga paratashimg* :: `3SG.F A hurt friend-N-1SG.POSS NOM tolerate-POT.ATTR-G.REVS`
		* ~"The fact that she hurt my friend is intolerable"
			* *parata* or *paratangu* mean 'rude' (negative toleration)
				* *parata* alone is fine, sometimes even *para* can be used depending on context
			* but *paratashimg* means 'intolerable' and is stronger, even though it does not require a degree
				* this is fine because 'intolerable' already naturally implies that it is something you cannot let go
* But **X ga/a/o Y ni Z** is used if **X ga Y** is the topic or theme:
	* *hashiru a hahaen haruma ieji ni eǵi* :: `child A entertain-VBZ hole AUG-LOC TOP okay-REVS-ATTR`
		* ~"The children playing near the hole is anything but OK"

## 3.2 Recursion
* *me/ni* is used for this
* If there is a *ni* already present, you're forced to use *me*
	* *ngoshi ni kamu atora ga nandanai tsu dan me unsunai* :: `bird TOP eat.V apple NOM fall-MID tree ABL REL live.V-MID`
		* ~"As for the bird, eat apple fall from tree, as for it, lives"
	* *Maria, Debwa a hangarapai ga naiwanq saoe me eanaite asasan* :: `maria debra A fight-REFL.MID NOM happy-REVS 3SG.F-N REL MOV-MID-INF house-3SG.F.POSS`
		* lit. "Maria and Debra fought with each other saddened her that move her home"
		* ~"The fact that Maria and Debra were fighting with each other made her sad enough to go back to her home"
* Tawa particularly dislikes this kind of recursion and nesting, so you would say:
	* *Sa ni Maria, Debwa a hangarapai ga naiwanqte eanaite asasan* :: `3SG.F TOP maria debra A fight-REFL.MID NOM happy-REVS-INF MOV-MID-INF house-3SG.F.POSS`
		* lit. "As for her, Maria and Debra fighting saddened (her) to go to her home"
		* ~"Maria and Debra fighting with each other made her sad, so she went home"
			* the *-te* here acts as a connective
			* *naiwanqte* means 'made sad, so' or 'made sad to'
			* *eanaite* refers to 'going to'
			* and *asasan* is the ultimate location


## 3.3 Clausal description
* *me* is also used for this, but often paired with a copular:
	* *hon me korumasu i* :: `person REL kill.V-AGNR COP`
		* "The person that is a killer"
	* *ii hon a kitate yume ribu me iinq i* :: `good person A like-INF read book REL good-ANT COP`
		* ~"The person that likes to read books is (actually) bad"
			* when you declare something as **X** but refer to that thing as **Y** later in the clause with *me*, it implies a surprising contrast
* Do note that the copular may be omitted in favour of an attributive, so:
	* *hon me korumashi*
* ... is better to say

## 3.4 Embedding
* Reported speech is always said as a statement followed by a 'speech' verb, like *fofu*:
	* *reoyaranai arenei wanō, so fofu* :: `IPFV-come-MID different-ATTR time speak.V`
		* "(He) will come at another time, he said"
* Use *eu* to indicate second-hand reporting of something:
	* *toniru eu asa ni lili mani i* :: `PFV-see.V COMP house TOP little money COP`
		* "I saw that the house was of little money"
			* this implies that you 'saw' it second-hand, so maybe through a picture, maybe indirectly, maybe you imagined it:
			* this is what you might say afterwards if you imagined it:
				* *mo mo eirra* :: `but ANAPH-NEG`
					* '... but it wasn't'
