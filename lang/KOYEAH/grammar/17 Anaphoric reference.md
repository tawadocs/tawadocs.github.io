## 17.1 Anaphoric reference
* NSKT is simple and easy, but this topic is, no doubt, quite complex
* In Tawa, a particle *ei* exists to refer to the latest or most relevant proposition:
	* *Kita nigōe* :: I like cats
	* *Ei wa* :: Me too / I also like cats

## 17.2 Basics
* Think of *ei* as a mental shortcut that expands to:
	* 'whatever was just said' / 'do'
* So:
	* *Kopa we tein no ohira ga buronzhi* :: the alloy of copper and tin is bronze
		* note: *ohira* is the default classifier for mixture, which includes, but is not limited to:
			* cocktails
			* smoothies
			* alloys
	* *Kitarra eioe* :: (I) dislike whatever I just said
* Without reading what I wrote below, try to guess what it means
* Here's what it means:
	* I dislike bronze (which is the alloy of copper and tin)
* If you guessed correctly, you've grokked anaphoric reference
* Another example:
	* *Mo mo, sa ga korumasu, shirenai no?* :: But I think she's a murderer
	* *Sa no yo? Eeeeeh! Watada einq ni haki unsonan ji* :: Her? Ha! Only anything but that is true in our lives
		* lit. 'Her? Ha! Only the opposite of what you just said is true in our life'

## 17.2 Anaphoric negation
* You only need to negate the correct part of the phrase:
	* *Kikita nigōe!* :: (I) love cats
	* *(Su ni) eirra* :: I don't
		* lit. 'whatever was just said is negated for me'
* Some examples:
	* *Kama no hataka ga makumatasu* :: The moon is beautiful
		* lit. 'The face of the moon can make (me) cry'
			* *-matasu* is used since *maku* (cry)
	* *Ei wa!* :: Me too!
		* lit. 'As for whatever you said, also'
	* *Aa kitanyu sa, sa ga iirra ue* :: Even though I love her, she isn't good for me
	* *Rri, eirri*
		* lit. 'Yes, as for what was just said (it is affirmable)'

### 17.2.1 Practicality
* Anaphoric reference isn't always practical, especially in fast-paced talks or debates (e.g., polemics)
* *ei* is like a mental shortcut that expands to: **whatever I just said that was most relevant**
* So:
	* *Ei ni...* :: as for whatever I just said (that was most relevant)... (X)
	* *Ei ga...* :: whatever I just said (that was most relevant)... (X)
	* *Eiǵ* :: the opposite of whatever I just said
* ... can be very taxing for learners or even seasoned speakers when they are used a lot, especially in complex clauses
* Also, these shortcuts are usually considered rude with strangers, so:
	* *fui kitarra ryo chingu no waoi, oto yo?* :: `ACT-ATTR like-NEG DEM.MED water-SRC.LEX GEN appease WH Q2`
		* ~"Could it be that (you) really don't like the taste of this drink?"
	* *Eirri* :: `ANAPH-AFFIRM`
		* this is rude, impolite, and immature
		* it can also be severely hurtful, as the speaker used *oto yo?*, which is an extremely polite softener
		* it is better to say:
			* *Rra, tanoe uerra. watada... mha amgchi, ngopo o nukunai.*
				* `NEG reason-N COP.POL-NEG | ONLY PRF FULL-water intestine U heart.PLAT.V-MID`
				* ~"No, (that) isn't the reason. I just feel like my insides are already full of water"
					* copular negation is better than directly negating *tanoe* (reason)
					* if you said *tanoerra*, it would sound less polite, and more like a 'you're wrong'
					* when you say *tanoe uerra*, you're essentially saying 'the reason doesn't exist'
					* it may feel grammatically off but it is more of an idiomatic shortcut
					* secondly, using *ngopo o nukunai* softens the reason, since it is the intestines (your gut) that is 'full of water'
					* here, *amgchi* (full of water) means that you're fully satiated or that you don't want to drink anymore
					* it has a slight 'I feel like I drank too much' feeling (depending on tone and context, of course!)
					* it describes the feeling when one drinks **too much water** or when one doesn't feel like drinking anything
					* of course, if you were actually sick, you'd say:
						* *ochi a, ngopo o aishinai, wou yoe!*
							* ~"The bloating is really hurting my intestines!"
							* *wou yoe!* is what adds the expression of unexpected pain
