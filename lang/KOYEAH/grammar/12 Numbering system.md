## 12.2 Operation
* *jou* or other place numbers are either multiplicative or additive, depending on placement:
	* *zu**jou*** :: **2x10** (20)
	* ***ji**zu* :: **10+2** (12)
* *′iyo* + *jou* forms *′ijou*, not *′iyojou*, so keep this in mind
	* it does this for other multiplicative structures too (e.g., *mga* :: *′imga*)
* In the *jiyo* form, it wouldn't do this, so, for instance, *jiyo* + *rre* would always be *jiyorre*

## 12.4 Format
* The format for writing the compounds for numbers is as follows:
	* `[PARENT]-[CHILD]'([CHILD2])...)`
		* let's apply it to *sawinicasega-nimgasejini*:
			* *sawi* :: parent (600K)
			* *nica* :: child descriptor 1 (90,000)
			* *sega* :: child descriptor 2 (6,000)
			* *nimga* :: parent 2 (900)
			* *sejini* :: child 1 of p2 (69)
				* *sejini* cannot be broken down further
				* *jini* alone means 19
				* *se* just means 6
				* if interpreted literally, this would be 619, but according to the rules of the system as previously described, *sejini* is 69
					* the pattern is **X-ji-Y**, where *X-* is the first tenth and *-Y* is the final
					* **-ji-** just connects *X-* to *-Y*
			* here:
				* *sawi* takes *nica, sega* as children
				* *nimga* takes *sejini* as a child
			* so:
				* *sawi-nica'sega nimga-sejini*

## 12.5 Morphosyntactic category
* A number can be:
	* **cardinal**
		* `number [appropriate linking particle] (...)`
			* in sets, *no* is used (X's Y / Y of X)
				* *watada... jou no zu no ni ii ue...*
					* only... two of/from the ten are good
			* for approximate quantities, *-bu* is used
				* *nggg, zubu... no?*
					* like... (is it) two-ish?
			* for exact quantities, *-te* is used
				* *kite nigo*
					* seven cats
	* **ordinal**
		* `([appropriate determiner]) number ([nu]) (...)`
			* usually, *ryo* is used unless the distal (*lyo*) or proximal (*byo*) aspect has to be emphasised
				* *ryo wan nigo ni subunai, fuka!* :: `DEM.MED one cat TOP desire.V-MID`
					* ~"I want the first cat, please!"
			* otherwise, *-nu* can be used to assign the number as a fixed property or title
				* *subute arenenai nemusunoe zhi byo... wannu nigo a ushikitsurra ue* :: `desire.V-INF change-MID decide-1SG.POSS-N TEMP DEM.PROX one-MOD cat A fun-NEG COP.POL`
					* ~"Never mind, the first cat wasn't all that fun"
					* lit. "(I) want to change my mind now, first cat is not fun"
				* *kinu nigo* :: `seven-MOD cat`
					* "The seven-cat"
						* maybe it has seven paws
						* maybe this cat is a part of a band named *ki* (seven)
						* or maybe you just want to vaguely characterise it by the number seven
					* "The seventh cat / cat #7"
	* **nominal**
		* `number + (vowel)n`
			* a doubled vowel plus *n* at the end of a number makes it a proper noun representing the digit
				* *kiwinq aiko! jiennai wan-zūn-kīn-awīn...* :: `continue-REVS IMP.POL LOC-VBZ-MID one-two.N-seven.N-one.hundred.thousand.N`
					* ~"Wait! (I'm) putting (the number) 1-2-7-100,000 (in)..."

### 12.5.1 Unmarked modification
* Especially after Tanji (including both Native Tanji and Kanji relexifications), numbers can function similarly to the English borrowed affixes like **mono**, **bi/di**, etc.:
	* *pan* (bread) + *zu* (two) :: *panzu* (sandwich)
	* *panwu*, similarly, would be a clubhouse sandwich
* Ashi Tawa prefixing for numbers is no longer used, so:
	* *Nyanguzu* :: `ROM.F-SRC.LEX-two`
		* lit. "bi-gendered romance"
			* *nyangu* refers to romantic gender (sexual attraction)
			* *zu* means 'two'
			* together, this means 'two romantic genders' or 'bisexual'
	* is used instead of older constructions
	* You could also stack Tanji:
		* *Manyangu* :: `three.drops.of.water-ROM.F-SRC.LEX`
			* lit. "three drops of water romantic gender"
			* ~"Fluid sexuality"
				* it can refer to pansexuality or sexualities that aren't consistent / rigid
* **Note:**
	* sexuality terms in Tawa are modern approximations
		* you'd use them in modern discourse if required, otherwise you usually never would
	* in Tawish culture, sexuality is not recognised as a social construct (i.e., a gay man simply *is*, and he is not put into a 'label' known as a gay)

## 12.6 Irregular number
* The most irregular number is one
* It has two forms:
	* *su*
	* *wan*
* *wan* can be used as a verb, noun, cardinal, ordinal - you name it
	* *so we sa ni su a wanunai* :: `3SG.M ADD 3SG.F TOP 1SG A unite.V-MID`
		* lit. "As for him and her, I united (them)"
		* ~"I (was the one that) united him and her"
* *su* can only be used as a digit noun, cardinal, and ordinal
* However, depending on the following cases, you'll have to choose *wan* over *su* or vice versa:
	* **digit proper nouns**
		* *wan* is used
			* *wan, zūn, wūn* (one, two, three)
	* **cardinals**
		* *wan* is used both in sets (with *no*) and approximates (*wambu*)
		* for exact quantities (with *-te*), *su* is used (*sute*)
			* *sute nigo* (one cat)
	* **ordinals**
		* *wange* or demonstrative pattern
			* *wannge nigo* (the first cat)
			* *ryo wan nigo* (the first cat)
		* *wan* alone would be used as a generaliser
			* *wan nigo* (some cat, a cat, one cat, another cat)

## 12.7 Shorthand number compounds
* Instead of complex number compounds, such as *sawi-nica'sega nimga-sejini* (696,969), you can:
	1. **break them up into digestible chunks**
		* *696,969* becomes *69-69-69*:
			* *sejini-sejini-sejini* (sixty-nine-sixty-nine-sixty-nine)
			* *sejini no wūn* (three of sixty-nines)
			* *wute sēnjinīn* (three sixty-nines)
			* *wute sēn-nīn* (three six-nines)
				* better to avoid this; it can be ambiguous
	2. **nominalise**
		* you can use *oe*, *no*, and the standard digit nominalisation methods:
			* *wute sēnjinīn* :: three sixty-nines
			* *wute sējinīn* :: three sixty-nines
				* both are fine, they vary speaker-to-speaker
				* this is the formal and polite method
				* you'd use this with strangers, higher-ups, sometimes even your elders
			* *wute sejinioe* :: three sixty-nines
				* less preferable than formal nominalisation
				* if saving time, this can be used
				* it is polite but slightly less formal than the former method
			* *wute sejini no* :: three sixty-nines
				* most informal method
				* can be jarring or impolite to strangers
