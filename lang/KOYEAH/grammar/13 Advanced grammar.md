## 13.1 Topic-comment structure
* Generally, this structure is used for complex thought:
	* `TOPIC COMMENT (CLAUSE / OPINION)`
		* *Subeki ryo komunishimg ni ieiukonq, riwakanai* :: `all.ATTR DEM.MED policy-unit-G.REVS TOP AUG-sound-REVS think-MID`
			* lit. "All this policy, as for (it), unsounder, think"
			* ~"I think that all of these policies are quite unfair"
* Examples:
	* *Sa no ni, hakuhwo i no?* :: `3SG NMZ TOP real-essence COP Q`
		* lit. "As for her, the reality is...?"
		* ~"How serious are you about her?"
			* *sa no* is used to turn *sa* (she) into an entire 'person'
			* saying *sa ni hakuhwo...* could be rude/degrading, since you're treating *sa* as a 'part' of a 'whole'
	* *Yada, fofu no?* :: `so speak.V Q`
		* lit. "So, speak?"
		* ~"Well, then do you wanna talk?"
		* ~"We should talk"
		* ~"Come on, I think we should talk"
			* you can drop entire clauses, postpositional phrases, or purposives as long as context makes it clear
	* *Ngggg, hakunu no?* :: `FIL real-MOD Q`
		* lit. "Hmmm, really?"
		* ~"Hmmmm, is that true?"
		* ~"Are you sure?"
		* ~"You for real?"
			* *hakui* is avoided since *hakui no* is actually a nominalised adjective, meaning 'the truthful thing / the real thing / thing of reality'
			* this is a rule for attributives
			* *-nu* does not form this with *no*
			* so instead of *hakui no yo?* (not preferred), you say *hakunu no?*
				* the former reads more literal, whereas the latter is more fluent and natural
	* *Zu a fofu no ni, arene i, toshire* :: `2SG A speak.V NMZ TOP different COP PFV-learn`
		* lit. "You said (as a thing), different is, learnt"
		* ~"I realised that what you said is different"
		* ~"I've learnt that what you said was different"

### 13.1.1 Experience or judgement
* Compare:
	* *Su ni, lyo a toii ue, riwakarranai* :: `1SG TOP DEM.DIST A PFV-good COP.POL think-NEG-MID`
		* lit. "As for me, that was good, (I) don't think so"
		* ~"I don't think that did good regarding me"
	* *Su ni, lyo o toii ue, riwakarranai* :: `1SG TOP DEM.DIST U PFV-good COP.POL think-NEG-MID`
		* lit. "As for me, that didn't undergo good, (I) don't think so"
		* ~"I didn't think that was good"
* So, what's the difference? Let's look at a simpler sentence:
	* *Lyo (a/o) toii, riwakanai* :: `DEM.DIST A/U PFV-good think-MID`
		* ~"That was good, I think"
* Dissecting it...
	* *lyo a toii, riwakanai*
		* that (thing) is good (as a property of it), I think
		* the comment *toii* is a property of *lyo*
		* you're judging *lyo*, the thing, itself
	* *lyo o toii, riwakanai*
		* that (thing) suffers good / is good, I think
		* the comment is about undergoing or experiencing *toii*
* Look at it in another example:
	* *sa o ii* :: `3SG.F U good COP.POL`
		* ~"She is good"
			* here, *o ii* implies:
				* she's naturally a good person
	* *sa a ii* :: `3SG.F A good`
		* ~"She is good"
			* here, *a ii* implies:
				* she's making a conscious effort of being good
				* she is continuously 'being good'

### 13.1.2 Anaphoric negation
* *einq ni nqi* :: `ANAPH-REVS TOP REVS-ATTR`
	* this is like saying, "as for whatever I just said before, (but) the opposite"
	* or "as for whatever is most relevant most pertaining to this conversation, this is the opposite contrast"
* Example:
	* *nigo ni kita, ei ni nqi* :: `cat TOP like ANAPH TOP REVS-ATTR`
		* ~"I like cats, but not dogs"
	* *kita zu, ei ni nqi* :: `like 2SG ANAPH TOP REVS-ATTR`
		* ~"(I) like you, but I dislike myself"
* You can also use it in constructions like:
	* *Wan teteson ga iinq i, einq ni ieiibu* :: `one hand-3SG.M.POSS NOM good-REVS COP ANAPH-REVS TOP AUG-good-PROP`
		* lit. "One hand of his is bad, the opposite of what I just said, as for it, kind of better"
		* ~"One of his hands is bad but the other one is a little bit better"
* Remember, *einq* reverses the whole *wan teteson ga iinq i*, so it becomes:
	* the other hand of his is good
* but when we restate *ni ieiibu*, it changes the state from *iinq i* to *ieiibu* (a little bit / kind of better)

## 13.2 Clausal actors
* A whole predicate can be an 'actor:'
	* *kanatashimg makure a fofu hakuhwōe* :: `word-POT.ATTR-G.REVS emotion A speak.V real-essence-N`
		* lit. "Wordable emotion speaks truth"
		* ~"Emotions that can't be put into words are the most truthful / genuine"
			* notice: *tasu* + *mg* forms *tasumg* (negative potential)
			* *tasu* + *i* forms *tashi* (potential attributive)
			* *tasu* + *i* + *mg* forms *tashimg* (negative potential attributive)
			* since *i* (the attributive) can't take *mg*, *mg* grammatically reverses *tasu*
			* this is an exception you have to remember

## 13.3 Clausal modification
* *no*, the genitive, can be used to modify clauses:
	* *Jijinu makure no futasumg kana ji a fofu hakuhwōe imnyi hon*
		* `INTENS~LOC-MOD emotion GEN ACT-POT-G.REVS word LOC A speak.V real-essence-N SIM.AUG.SUP person `
		* lit. "Undoable in words of absolute emotion speaks reality the same as a person"
		* ~"Emotions that cannot be put into words are the very things that tell the truth of what it means to be human"
			* *hakuhwōe imnyi hon* means 'reality the same as a person' (i.e., what it means to be human)
			* here, *jijinu makure* is a phrase meaning 'exact/precise emotion'
			* *no* after *jijinu makure* allows it to modify *futasumg kana ji* (undoable in words / cannot put into words)
			* so *jijinu makure no futasumg kana ji* means 'precisely the emotions that cannot be put into words'
				* more literally: **precise emotions' undoable/undoability in words**

## 13.4 Postpositional infinitives
* Verbs like *ea* hate postpositions in their middle voice form:
	* *eanaite kamu* :: (I) go out to eat
		* but if you said:
			* *eanai kam**u** ji*
		* then this doesn't parse well because *kamu* is a verb (to eat)
		* you'd use *eanai kam**en** ji* (I went out for eating)
			* this is formal, and not something you'd say with a friend or acquaintance
		* you could also say *eanai kamu un ji* (I went for at eating), which is grammatically correct, **but its a bit like saying something like such as similar to like what I just said**
* *-te* is especially even more preferable when you need to use a clause as the purposive of another:
	* *Tosubute eanai hariasa jite nirunaimg ryo honoe, lyo atora ji* :: `PFV-desire.V-MID MOV-MID exchange-house LOC-INF show.V-MID-G.REVS DEM.MED person-N DEM.DIST apple LOC`
		* ~"I waned to go to the store to show this person that apple over there"
			* here, *ji* marks *tosubute eanai hariasa* as the purposive, and *te* attributes it to *niru ryo honoe lyo atora ji*

## 13.5 Demonstratives and postpositions
* Use *un* for location of action:
	* *urukuennnai lyo un* :: `stand-VBZ-MID DEM.DIST INSTR`
		* ~"Standing over there"
* Use *ji* for location of existence:
	* *urukuennai lyo ji* :: `stand-VBZ-MID DEM.DIST LOC`
		* ~"Standing in (over) there"
* Or omit the case and let context do the work, since *uruku* by default accepts another argument that describes the **where**:
	* *urukuen byōe* :: `stand-VBZ DEM.PROX-N`
		* ~"Standing here"
	* *urukuen ryōe* :: `stand-VBZ DEM.MED-N`
		* ~"Standing at this point / there (near us)"

## 13.6 **au** constructions
* *au* affects the subject, but it is never marked with *-nai*, since it is irregular
* Partially, this is a reason that *o* is used over *o* with *au* if *au* is marking existence, like:
	* *O au sa...* :: `U HAVE.V 3SG.F`
		* ~"I have her..."
* But *a* is used if this 'existence' becomes active:
	* *Su a reoau subeko no mani hiroga do* :: `1SG A HAVE.V all GEN money world PER`
		* ~"I will get all the money in the world!"
	* *Su a subute au epikui tahete!* :: `1SG A desire.V-INF HAVE.V epic-ATTR electronic`
		* ~"I want to get an epic electronic"
* You can also use *ni* without *a*:
	* *ngoshi ni au kamo* :: the bird has food
* But as soon as you want to say 'get,' you use *a*:
	* *ngoshi a au kamo* :: the bird gets / obtains food
* Or you could use the modern / informal term *demg*:
	* *ngoshi ni demg kamo* :: the bird gets food
		* *demg* (formerly *denh*) is formed from *de*, which is the receiving relational, and the grammatical reverser *-mg*
		* *aumg* is the proper Koyeah Tawa variant, but *aumg* can only be used with *ga*
			* use *demg* when you also aren't using *ga*
			* use *aumg* when you are using *ga*
* Here are some constructions where *au* does something unique:
	* *Sa o au iitasu* :: `3SG.F U HAVE.V good-POT`
		* lit. "She has goodability"
		* ~"She might be good ((after all), you never know)"
	* *Shirerranai, ya! So o au korumasutasu* :: `know-NEG-MID ASSERT | 3SG.M U HAVE.V kill.V-AG-POT`
		* ~"I don't know! He might be a killer for all you know"

## 13.7 Mood copular constructions
* Also a part of advanced grammar, the mood copular constructions, such as:
	* *itai*
	* *iare*
	* *inatai*
		* want / simply long, less stronger due to diminutive *-suta*
	* *irranatai*
		* note: *nai* drops the *i* when pairing with *atai*, which drops its *a*
		* the copular also connects to *atai* as *itai*, where *atai* drops the *a*
* ... are used for highly romantic or emotional constructions
* For example:
	* *zu ni su mo inatai anano?* :: `2SG TOP 1SG COM COP-MID.DES AFFEC-Q`
		* ~"Do you emotionally long for us to be together?"
		* ~"I want you and me to be together"
			* but its more emotional than that
	* *zu ni su mo iare no?* :: `2SG TOP 1SG COM COP-OPT Q`
		* ~"Don't you wish that we were together?"
		* ~"Shouldn't we be together?"
		* ~"Isn't it better if we were together?"
		* ~"Ever wish that you and I were together?"

## 13.8 Determiners
### 13.8.1 **Many** and **some**
* **Pattern**
	* `myo no X` = many X
		* `mymyo no X` = most (of) X
	* `myomg no X` = some X
		* `mymyomg no X` = least (of) X
* Examples:
	* *Myo no atora* :: many apples
	* *Myomg no atora* :: a few / some apples

### 13.8.2 **Any** and **little**
* **Pattern for any**
	* `we no X` = any (of) X
		* *byo ji ni we no ng i*
			* ~"you can find anything here"
		* *earranaite we no ng*
			* ~"I'm not going anywhere"
	* `wewe no X` = every nook and cranny of X
	* `wemg no X` = none (of) X
		* *byo ji ni wemg no ng i*
			* ~"there is nothing here"
		* *eanaite wemg no ng*
			* ~"I'm going to the middle of nowhere"
			* ~"I'm going to a place nobody knows about"
			* ~"Where I'm going is none of your business"
	* `wewemg no X` = not a single trace of X
* **Pattern for little**
	* `lili no X` = a little X
		* e.g., *lili mani no X* = poor/impoverished X

### 13.8.3 **Wh-ever** words
* **Whatever, whichever, whenever, wherever**
	* `we no ng` (whatever/whichever)
		* *fu we no ng* :: do whatever
		* *kamu we no ng* :: eat whatever/whichever
	* `zhi we no ng` or `we no wanō` (whenever)
		* *kamu zhi we no ng* :: eat whenever/anytime
		* *fu yaranai, we no wanō* :: do come, anytime
	* `-te/(jite) we no ng` (wherever)
		* *kamute we no ng* :: eat wherever
		* *haterunaite we no ng* :: run wherever
			* note: *haterunai* is used because *hateru* means **to trample**
			* so:
				* *sorama a hateru sōe* :: rain trampled over him / ran him over
				* *sorama a haterunai* :: the rain runs
				* *sa a haterunaite sōe* :: she ran after him
* The same patterns are used for:
	* **anyone, anything, anywhere, anytime**

### 13.8.4 **Every** and **all**
* **Patterns for every and all**
	* `subeko no X` :: all (of) X
		* *subeko no wanō* :: eternity / forever (literally 'all of time')
			* cf. *we no wanō* :: anytime
		* *subeko no hon* :: all humans
		* *subeko no kitei* :: all things
	* `subeki X` :: every X
		* *subeki ribu* :: every book
		* *subeki hon* :: every human
		* *subeki kitei* :: everything
	* `subekinu X` :: all pertaining to X
		* *subekinu ribu* :: anything related to books (e.g., pens and paper)
		* *subekinu hon* :: anything related to humans (e.g., life, organs)
		* *subekinu chi* :: anything related to water (e.g., liquid, drinks, solvent, cups)
