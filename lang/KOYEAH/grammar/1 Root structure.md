## 1.1 Sentences
* Sentence structure is SVO
* Modifiers go before the noun
	* *meiya (soft) ngoshi (bird)* -> soft bird
* Topic-comment structure
* Context-first and pro-drop

## 1.2 The nine core particles
| Particle | IPA       | Gloss   |
| -------- | --------- | ------- |
| ni       | `/ni/`    | TOP     |
| a        | `/a/`     | A       |
| o        | `/o/`     | U       |
| nai      | `/nai/`   | MID     |
| nq       | `/ŋ~ɴ/`   | ANT     |
| te       | `/te/`    | INF     |
| no       | `/no/`    | GEN / Q |
| ra       | `/ɺa~ɾa/` | NEG     |
| ri       | `/ɺi~ɾi/` | AFFIRM  |
* **ni** marks the topic:
	* *atora ni kamu* :: `apple TOP eat.V`
		* lit. "As for the apple, (I) eat (it)"
		* ~"I eat the apple"
* **a** marks the actor:
	* *atora a kamu* :: `apple A eat.V`
		* "The apple eats"
* **o** marks the undergoer:
	* *sa o koru* :: `3SG.F U kill.V`
		* "She is killed"
* Notice:
	* *atora **ni** hon kamu* :: `apple TOP person eat.V`
		* "As for the apple, the person eats (it)"
	* *atora **a** hon kamu* :: `apple A person eat.V`
		* "The apple humanises food"
	* *atora **o** hon kamu* :: `apple U person eat.V`
		* "The person eats the apple"
		* ~"The apple is eaten by the person"
* **nai** is the middle voice:
	* *sa o korunai* :: `3SG.F U kill.V-MID`
		* "She dies"
		* no killer is implied, since *nai* reduces the amount of arguments a verb can take, thus decreasing valency
	* *omoko o kamunai* :: `chicken U eat.V-MID`
		* "The chicken is eaten"
* **nq** is used for antonymisation:
	* *kamunq* :: `eat.V-ANT`
		* ~"Vomit"
	* *kamonq* :: `food-ANT`
		* ~"Inedible (substance)"

### 1.2.1 Focus
* Usage of **ni**
	* It introduces the topic; i.e., what the whole sentence is about
	* It focuses the information after the particle
	* Example:
		* *atora ni mafo i* :: `apple TOP red COP`
			* The apple is old news, but the 'red' part is new information
* Usage of **a**
	* It marks the subject performing a deed
	* The focus is on the noun before the particle
	* Example:
		* *ngoshi a kamu* :: `bird A eat.V`
			* The eating isn't the news; the fact that a bird doing it is
* Usage of **o**
	* It marks the subject suffering a deed
	* Example:
		* *omoko o kamu* :: `chicken U eat.V`
			* The chicken experiences eating
			* This doesn't mean that the chicken is being eaten (you would use *kamonai* for that), but that 'eating' happens to it

### 1.2.2 Nuances of using **a** / **o**
* *a* often implies a specific subject (it is X and only X)
	* *a kamu* :: `A eat.V`
		* "The actor and only the actor eats"
* *ni* often implies contrast (as for X, not Y)
	* *omoko ni kamu* :: `chicken TOP eat.V`
		* "(I usually) eat chicken, (not beef / red meat)"
	* *ryo ni ii, lyo ni iinq* :: `DEM.MED TOP good DEM.DIST TOP good-INV`
		* This is good, but that isn't good

## 1.3 O-roots
* For nasal-only clusters, *o-roots* drop the *′o* to take the consonant:
	* *kamo* + *-bu* :: *kambu*

## 1.4 Questions and genitive
* *no* is an interrogative when sentence-final but genitive when non-sentence-final:
	* *kanata no hon* :: `happy GEN person`
		* lit. "Happy's person"
		* ~"A person who is happy"
	* *hon no kanata* :: `person GEN happy`
		* lit. "Person's happiness / the happiness of the person"
	* *haku no kita* :: `truth GEN like`
		* "Genuine liking"
	* *jananq no hon* :: `heaven-ANT GEN person`
		* lit. "A person of hell"
		* ~"Dweller of hellfire"

## 1.5 Infinitive
- The infinitive _te_ marks infinitives
- It's uses are listed here:
    - **purpose** (intentional infinitive)
        - _fuyu wante paya no?_ -> `what one-INF study Q`
            - "Which one to study?"
        - _eate kamu_ -> `MOV-INF eat.V`
            - "Go to eat"
    - **complement of modal / auxiliary verbs**
        - _nasute paya_ -> `ability.V-INF study`
            - "Can study"
        - _anisute paya_ -> `requirement.V-INF study`
            - "Must study"
        - _subute paya_ -> `desire.V-INF study`
            - "Want to study"
    - **complement of perception / causative verbs**
        - _kitate nirute so o korunai_ -> `like-INF see.V-INF 3SG.M U die.V-IV`
            - "(I would) like to see him (be) killed / die"
        - _rute hashirusun kamu_ -> `CAUS.V-INF child-1SG.POSS eat.V`
            - "Make my child eat"
    - **subject or object in nominal constructions**
        - _payate ni ushikitsu i_ -> `study-INF TOP fun COP`
            - "Studying is fun / to study is fun"
        - _ryo ni payate i_ -> `DEM.MED TOP study-INF COP`
            - "This is for studying"
        - _ryo ni iite paya i_ -> `DEM.MED TOP good-INF study COP`
            - "This is good to study"
    - **description**
        - _ushikitsute paya ita ei_ -> `fun-INF study PRTV ANAPH`
            - "(It is) fun to study about this"
        - _damite kamu_ -> `easy-INF eat.V`
            - "Easy to eat"
        - _iite fu_ -> `good-INF ACT`
            - "Good to do"

## 1.6 Polarity
* *ra* and *ri* are both used to negate and affirm, respectively:
	* *Yararanai* = did not come
	* *Yararinai* = (indeed) came
* They are not only suffixes:
	* *Ri, lyo ni haku i* = yeah, that's true
	* *Ra, su fofu eu arene i* = no, what I said is different
	* *Ra, su fofura ryoe!* = no, I didn't say this!
		* for reported speech, what is being reported is nested with **me**
		* so 'he said he's epic' is *so fofu **me** so ni epiku* (he says (that) he is epic)
			* for quotations, just use *mo*:
				* *so fofu mo 'su ni epiku'* (he said, "I am epic")
	* *Riwakara ita ei, ra...* = didn't think about that, no...

## 1.7 Similatives
* All similative particles are the same as Ashi Tawa's
* System is used in the same manner (e.g., *osarara su imnyi so* - 'don't call me him!' is fine in Koyeah Tawa)

## 1.8 Omitting particles
* Never omit *ni*, but you may omit the topic itself if clear from context:
	* *To, ni so a ai* :: `PFV TOP 3SG.M A help`
		* lit. "As for (me), he helped"
		* ~"He helped (me)"
			* the pronoun *su* is omitted, but the topic remains
* The actor particle can be omitted many times:
	* *To, ni so ai* :: `PFV TOP 3SG.M help`
		* "He helped me"
			* here, there are two things that help ensure the phrase is parsable:
				* *so ai* comes before the topic (we now know this is a comment)
				* no copular (we now know this is not a description)
				* **pronoun + root** construction
					* a copularless comment comprising of a pronoun + root is, well, very likely an action
