## 15.1 Discourse particles
| Particle | IPA   | Gloss         |
| -------- | ----- | ------------- |
| yád     | `jæd` | so            |
| mo       | `mo`  | then          |
| ám      | `æm`  | INTERJ        |
| áni     | `æɲi` | ST.TOP.ANAPH  |
| áa      | `æa`  | ADV.AGT.ANAPH |
* **NOTE:** depending on context, *mo* can mean "than" too; *mo mo* means "but"
* Examples:
	* *yád, zua fofnnaito koronaisanita hya?* -> `so 2SG-AGT hear-PFV kill-IV-3SG.F.POSS-PRTV Q`
		* "So, did you hear about her death?"
	* *sua kamu mo kamu mo kamu ę mo... kamuthu ielénĉ* -> `1SG-AGT eat.V.PLAT then eat.V.PLAT then eat.V.PLAT and then eat.V.PLAT-PFV.V.PLAT AUG-INDEF-N.PLAT`
		* "I ate then ate then ate and then... ate some more"
	* *mo mo sua kitanyu zu* -> `but 1SG-AGT like-V.ROM 2SG`
		* "But, I love you"
	* *ám, sua fofuthu fuyu hyo..?* -> `INTERJ 1SG-AGT speak.V.PLAT-PFV.V.PLAT what OPEN.Q`
		* "Um, what was I saying?"

## 15.2 Phrases
* Some idiomatic expressions include:
	* *a de ĉisun aiko!* -> `AGT REC water-1SG.POSS IMP`
		* "Take my water!"
		* meaning: "no way / hold up / wait"
		* marks attention, a potential change in stance, and even disbelief
	* *yád, ĉisuno tara* -> `so water-1SG.POSS-AGT spill`
		* "Well, my water has been spilled"
		* meaning: "I'm done / I give up"
		* marks resignation or defeat
	* *mha kiwinq tetezun* -> `PRF stop hand-2SG.POSS`
		* "Just stop your hand"
		* meaning: "wait a second / calm down"
		* marks patience
	* *kamureu ĉizun* -> `eat.V.PLAT-IPFV.V.PLAT water-2SG.POSS`
		* "Will drink your water"
		* meaning: "to trust someone / to be ready to do something with someone"
		* marks trust and readiness

## 15.3 Anaphoric discourse particles
* *áa* is used for subject-oriented anaphoric constructions where new context is needed, and where there may be an adversative mood needed
* *áni* is used for topic-oriented anaphoric constructions where new context has to be in a state equated with the original predicate
* Examples (difference of *áa* vs. *áni*):
	* *sua kamuto. áa sorala ĉiru* -> `1SG-AGT eat-PFV ADV.AGT.ANAPH sky-DEF-AGT water-V.PLAT`
		* lit. "I ate. I did so even though the sky waters"
		* ~"I ate even though it rained"
	* *sua kamuto. áni sorala ĉiru* -> `1SG-AGT eat-PFV ST.TOP.ANAPH sky-DEF-AGT water-V.PLAT`
		* lit. "I ate. I did so whilst it rained for me"
			* this is in a literal sense
			* *"it rained for me"* here literally indicates that the speaker only experienced the rain and that the others did not
		* ~"I ate as it rained, but only for me"
* A more relevant use of *áni* is illustrated in this short dialogue:
	* A: *yád honlúa haterén ę haterén iyukryąllanh, ę su ni watada oĉikathu ryojią...*
		* `so person-DEF.PL-AGT run.NMLZ and run.NMLZ sound.this.PROX.MOD-ABL and 1SG TOP only stand-PFV.V.PLAT this.PROX-LOC-MOD`
			* "so the people ran and ran, away from this sound, and I just stood there..."
	* B: *zua watada oĉikathu hya?!*
		* `2SG-AGT only stand-PFV.V.PLAT Q`
			* "you just stood there?!"
	* A: *áni iyukryąa iyukurra*
		* `ST.TOP.ANAPH sound.this.PROX.MOD-AGT sound.V.PLAT-NEG`
			* "I did so whilst this sound did not play for me"
* An explanation of the dialogue:
	* *A* saw people running due to a sound, but he stood there
	* *B* asks him why
	* *A* explains that he never heard the sound
