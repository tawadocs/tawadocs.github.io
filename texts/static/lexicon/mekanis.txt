A loanword, meaning 'mechanics.' It refers to the branch of physics concerned with the relationships between force, matter, and motion amongst physical objects.

It is different from haitei (from hai (antiproduct verbaliser) + tei (product)) in that haitei refers to the specific, usually canonical, modus operandi of something, whereas mekanis is usually used in physics and engineering contexts concerned with the behaviour of physical bodies when subjected to forces or displacements, and the subsequent effects on their environment.

So, 'chi no mekanis' is like saying 'the mechanics of water' (i.e., its behaviour when subjected to a force and effect on the environment), whereas 'chi no haitei' is like saying 'the modus operandi of water' (i.e., transporting nutrients, acting as a universal solvent, etc.).
